Steel (Scalable Throughput-optimized Engine for Embodied Learning) is a GPU solver for articulated robots and rigid bodies in [Newton](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton). It integrates robots in joint coordinates, solves contacts and joint constraints with a projected Gauss-Seidel sweep that does not assemble the world's constraint matrix, and schedules every world of a batch on its own, so different robots, tasks and scenes can share one batch. Newton is the open-source physics engine hosted by the Linux Foundation.

<p class="chips"><span class="chip"><b>newton.solvers.SolverSteel</b></span><span class="chip">experimental in Newton 1.8</span></p>

We benchmark Steel against PhysX and MuJoCo Warp on single-task and mixed-task Isaac Lab workloads in the [launch post](https://www.skild.ai/blogs/steel).

## What it is for

Steel was built for robot learning at scale: thousands of environments per GPU, fixed memory, every step capturable in a <Term name="graph">CUDA graph</Term>, and batches that mix embodiments. Tree joints hold exactly because the state is the joint coordinates; constraint rows are needed only for contacts, limits, optional drive rows, mimic couplings and loop closures. In the default serial sweep each world's rows are solved by one GPU <Term name="warp">warp</Term> in sequence, which keeps <Term name="gs">Gauss-Seidel</Term>'s convergence while thousands of worlds run in parallel.

Steel is experimental in Newton 1.8: `SolverSteel`, its constructor options and its `steel:` attributes may change without the normal deprecation period.

## Newton's articulated-body solvers

Newton has several solvers that simulate articulated bodies. From their source and Newton's solver documentation, with Newton's joint types PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE, BALL, FIXED, FREE, DISTANCE, D6 and ROD:

| Solver | Coordinates | Contacts and constraints | Joint types | Loop closures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Steel**<br />`SolverSteel` | Reduced | Hard contacts with Coulomb friction; matrix-free projected Gauss-Seidel | All but ROD; DISTANCE moves as FREE | BALL, REVOLUTE and FIXED loop joints |
| **MuJoCo Warp**<br />`SolverMuJoCo` | Reduced | MuJoCo soft constraints (`solref`, `solimp`); CG or Newton solver; pyramidal or elliptic cone | All but DISTANCE and ROD | Loop joints become CONNECT or WELD equality constraints |
| **Featherstone**<br />`SolverFeatherstone` | Reduced | Penalty spring-damper contacts | All but ROD; DISTANCE moves as FREE | No |
| **Kamino**<br />`SolverKamino` | Maximal | Hard contacts with friction and restitution as a complementarity problem; proximal ADMM by default | PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE, BALL, FIXED, FREE | Yes |
| **XPBD**<br />`SolverXPBD` | Maximal | Position-based contact and joint constraints | All but ROD | Yes |
| **Semi-implicit**<br />`SolverSemiImplicit` | Maximal | Penalty spring-damper contacts and joints | All but ROD; DISTANCE moves as FREE | Yes |
| **VBD**<br />`SolverVBD` | Maximal | Augmented-Lagrangian contact and joint rows (AVBD) | All but DISTANCE | Yes |

| Solver | Particles, cloth, soft bodies | Gradients | Devices | Worlds with different structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Steel** | Through coupled solvers | No | CUDA; CPU for the core solve | Yes |
| **MuJoCo Warp** | Through coupled solvers | No | CUDA (MuJoCo Warp); CPU (MuJoCo C) | No: bodies, joints and equality constraints match across worlds; meshes may differ |
| **Featherstone** | In the same solver | Basic (diffsim examples) | CPU, CUDA | Yes |
| **Kamino** | Through coupled solvers | No | CPU, CUDA | Yes |
| **XPBD** | In the same solver | No | CPU, CUDA | Yes |
| **Semi-implicit** | In the same solver | Basic (diffsim examples) | CPU, CUDA | Yes |
| **VBD** | In the same solver | No | CPU, CUDA | Yes |

Newton marks Steel, Kamino and VBD experimental. Steel can be one entry of Newton's experimental coupled solvers, next to VBD or XPBD for cloth, cables and soft bodies; see [Coupling](/guides/coupling/). Model features Steel rejects at construction are listed in the [FAQ](/faq/#what-does-steel-reject-at-construction).

To see what Steel runs, browse the [examples](/examples/): scenes with a clip of each one's Steel preset, its presets for the other solvers and the command that runs it.
