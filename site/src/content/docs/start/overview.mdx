Steel is a GPU solver for articulated robots and rigid bodies in [Newton](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton). It integrates robots in joint coordinates, solves contacts and joint constraints with a projected Gauss-Seidel sweep that does not assemble the world's constraint matrix, and schedules every world of a batch on its own, so different robots, tasks and scenes can share one batch.

<p class="chips"><span class="chip"><b>newton.solvers.SolverSteel</b></span><span class="chip">experimental in Newton 1.8</span><span class="chip">documented at skild-ai/newton <b>baffc2a</b></span></p>

## What it is for

Steel was built for robot learning at scale: thousands of environments per GPU, fixed memory, every step capturable in a <Term name="graph">CUDA graph</Term>, and batches that mix embodiments. Tree joints hold exactly because the state is the joint coordinates; constraint rows are needed only for contacts, limits, optional drive rows, mimic couplings and loop closures. In the default serial sweep each world's rows are solved by one GPU <Term name="warp">warp</Term> in sequence, which keeps <Term name="gs">Gauss-Seidel</Term>'s convergence while thousands of worlds run in parallel.

Steel is experimental in Newton 1.8: `SolverSteel`, its constructor options and its `steel:` attributes may change without the normal deprecation period.

## Choosing a solver

Newton ships several solvers. A rough guide for rigid and articulated scenes:

| Solver | Coordinates and contact model | Pick it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Steel** | Joint coordinates; hard contacts, limits and couplings as rows of a projected Gauss-Seidel solve; implicit drives | Many worlds of articulated robots with contact, including batches that mix different robots or scenes; reproducible runs (`RUN_TO_RUN`) |
| **MuJoCo Warp**<br />`SolverMuJoCo` | Joint coordinates; MuJoCo's soft-constraint contact model | You need MuJoCo semantics: its actuators, tendons, equality constraints and `solref`/`solimp`, or parity with an existing MuJoCo model |
| **XPBD**<br />`SolverXPBD` | Maximal coordinates; position-based constraints, joints included | Scenes with particles, cloth or soft bodies alongside rigid bodies |
| **Featherstone**<br />`SolverFeatherstone` | Joint coordinates; penalty (spring-damper) contacts | Basic differentiable simulation, or articulated scenes where penalty contact is acceptable |

Steel can also be one entry of Newton's experimental coupled solvers, next to VBD or XPBD for cloth, cables and soft bodies. See [Coupling](/guides/coupling/).

## What is supported

<CardGrid>

<InfoCard title="Supported">

- Joints: PRISMATIC, REVOLUTE, BALL, FIXED, D6, and root FREE and DISTANCE joints (a DISTANCE joint moves like a free joint)
- Implicit PD drives, or drives as solver rows with exact effort limits
- Joint position and velocity limits, passive springs, dry joint friction
- Mimic joints, and loop-closing BALL, REVOLUTE and FIXED joints
- Hard contacts with Coulomb friction, persistent friction patches, restitution, optional torsion
- Kinematic bodies, heterogeneous worlds, global bodies shared by all worlds
- CUDA graph capture of `step` and `reset`; run-to-run determinism; island sleeping
- CPU fallback for the core solve

</InfoCard>

<InfoCard title="Not supported">

- Particles, cloth and soft bodies: construction raises when the model has particles. Couple Steel with VBD or XPBD instead.
- ROD joints
- Newton's native routed tendons
- Gradients: construction raises for `model.requires_grad=True`
- Mimic couplings across articulations, and loop closures between two dynamic articulations
- On CPU: contact torsion, contact compliance, joint friction, Nesterov momentum and the non-default friction modes
- DISTANCE joint limits: `min_distance` and `max_distance` are ignored, also with `enable_joint_limits=True`
- MJCF `condim`: the importer leaves `steel:condim` at 3; set it after import

</InfoCard>

</CardGrid>
