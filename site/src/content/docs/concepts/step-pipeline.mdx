A step has two halves. The first runs once per articulation and is classical <Term name="crba">Featherstone dynamics</Term>, with a <Term name="chol">Cholesky</Term> factor of the mass matrix. The second runs once per world and turns contacts, limits, drives, mimic couplings and loop closures into rows of a velocity-level projected Gauss-Seidel (PGS) solve. Positions then follow from the solved velocities by symplectic Euler.

<StepPipelineFigure n={1} />

With joint-space inertia $H(q)$, bias forces $C(q,\dot q)$, applied generalized force $\tau$ and step $h$, the unconstrained velocity is

$$
\tilde H = H + \operatorname{diag}(\text{armature}) + \operatorname{diag}(h\,k_d + h^2 k_e),\qquad \hat v = \dot q + h\,\tilde H^{-1}\big(\tau + u_0 - C(q,\dot q)\big),
$$

where the extra diagonal folds the default implicit drives into the mass matrix and $u_0 = -k_e(q - q^* + h\dot q) - k_d(\dot q - \dot q^*)$ is the drive force at the start of the step, clamped to the effort limit. Constraint impulses $\lambda$ act through row Jacobians $J$, so $v^+ = \hat v + \tilde H^{-1}J^\top\lambda$. With $u = Jv^+$ the rows' physical velocity and $b$ their bias (position correction, speculative gaps and bounce targets), normal rows satisfy the <Term name="lcp">complementarity</Term> condition $0 \le \lambda_n \perp u_n + b_n \ge 0$ and friction rows stay in the Coulomb disk $\|\lambda_t\| \le \mu\lambda_n$.

The bias forces $C(q,\dot q)$ and the passive joint springs and damping in $\tau$ are taken at the start of the step, so they are explicit, as in SolverFeatherstone. That sets two limits on $h$:

- **Passive joint damping** $c$ is stable only while $h\,c\,\lambda_{\max}(\tilde H^{-1}) < 2$. Light links make $\lambda_{\max}$ large: for a six-link chain of 2 cm capsules it is about $4\times10^4$, so at 240 Hz damping above about 0.012 N·m·s/rad diverges. Drive gains do not have this limit, because they are folded into $\tilde H$. A change that folds passive damping into $\tilde H$ the same way is in review in [skild-ai/newton#18](https://github.com/skild-ai/newton/pull/18).
- **Velocity-product forces** of fast, light chains add energy at a rate that is first order in $h$; Featherstone and MuJoCo's `euler` integrator behave the same. Iterations do not help, since no rows are involved. A smaller $h$ reduces the gain; armature large against the links' inertia also does, but it changes the model.
