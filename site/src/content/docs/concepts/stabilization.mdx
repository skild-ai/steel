**Position error** is corrected through velocity only. A penetrating contact adds $\beta\phi/h$ to its target with $\beta$ = `baumgarte` (0.2). A separated contact within its gap adds $s\phi/h$ with $s$ = `contact_speculative_scale` (1), which lets it close exactly the gap in one step and blocks faster approach; these speculative contacts stand in for continuous collision detection at robot speeds. `velocity_iterations` adds sweeps without the position bias, so stabilization does not add velocity to bodies. `contact_regularization` turns penetrating rows into an implicit spring, which makes indeterminate load splits unique at a small resting penetration.

**Restitution** uses the closing speed at the start of the step: an impacting contact faster than `restitution_velocity_threshold` (0.5 m/s) gets the rebound target $u_n \ge -e\,u_\text{start}$. Resting contacts pressed by gravity or a drive never bounce. Shape restitution defaults to zero.

**Drives** are implicit by default (`drive_mode="augmented"`): $h k_d + h^2 k_e$ joins the mass-matrix diagonal, so stiff gains stay stable at ordinary steps. Only the explicit part is clamped to `joint_effort_limit`; the implicit reaction is not, so under a large external load the drive can exceed the limit. `drive_mode="rows"` solves each driven DOF as a row with the PhysX articulation drive update and clamps its impulse at the effort limit times $h$; below saturation and without contacts it converges to the implicit drive.

**Soft contact and adhesion.** `contact_compliance` solves hydroelastic contacts as implicit spring-dampers, and the per-shape `steel:adhesion` (integration branch) lets contacts pull within their gap.

<p class="tutlinks">Tutorials [Hydroelastic contact and compliance](/tutorials/hydroelastic-compliance/) · [Adhesion](/tutorials/adhesion/) · [Contact forces and regularization](/tutorials/table-legs/)</p>

**Joint limits are off by default.** With `enable_joint_limits=False` authored limits are not simulated. Turn them on whenever the model authors limits.

**Loop closures** are bilateral rows in the same sweep: three for BALL, five for REVOLUTE, six for FIXED. They read `joint_enabled`, `joint_X_p`, `joint_X_c` and the hinge axis every step, so a weld can be released or re-anchored mid-run, also between graph replays. `enable_bilateral_preelimination` removes most of their dependence on `iterations`.

Two smaller details: kinematic DOFs are handled inside the factorization with an armature of $10^{10}$, and single-body free objects replace the explicit gyroscopic term with an energy-preserving update over 1 to 32 adaptive microsteps, so spinning, thin objects do not gain energy.
