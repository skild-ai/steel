With `enable_sleeping=True`, articulations in contact are joined into islands each step. Static geometry supports an island without joining it; a fixed-base robot with moving joints joins the island of everything it touches. A supported island whose bodies stay below `sleep_linear_threshold` (0.05 m/s) and `sleep_angular_threshold` (0.15 rad/s) for `sleep_quiet_time` (0.5 s) falls asleep: its published state is frozen and, with `sleep_skip_constraints=True`, its rows and dynamics are skipped. Collision keeps running, so a contact with an awake or moving body wakes it.

- Other wake events: nonzero or non-finite `body_f` or `joint_f`, a changed state or gravity, lost support or row capacity, `reset` of its world, `notify_model_changed` for its properties.
- Driven articulations (nonzero drive gains) and articulations with passive springs stay awake.
- Sleeping is rejected with `contact_warmstart`, `velocity_iterations`, models without articulations, and serial propagation. With colored propagation on CUDA, a captured step in which everything that can sleep is asleep skips the propagation solve where conditional graph nodes are supported.
- Contacts of sleeping islands report zero in `contact_f` while their rows are skipped. Pass `sleep_skip_constraints=False` if force consumers read them.

**Replicated worlds.** The default collision pipeline writes the same contacts in a different order on every call, and sleeping rules out warm start, so each step's cold-started sweep leaves a slightly different residual. Resting bodies keep a small jitter whose fast tail reaches the default `sleep_angular_threshold`. An island must stay under the thresholds on every step of `sleep_quiet_time`, so whether it sleeps is very sensitive to that tail: in 16 to 128 copies of the tower scene from the [tutorial](/tutorials/sleeping/), most bodies stayed awake after 6 s, and the outcome varied between runs and did not change monotonically with the world count. Nothing couples the worlds. `CollisionPipeline(deterministic=True)` sorts the contacts, which roughly halves the jitter, and every island sleeps within a second or two; a larger `sleep_angular_threshold`, such as 0.3 rad/s, also works. Check `body_awake` in batched runs before counting on the saving. Solver documentation of this is in review in [skild-ai/newton#25](https://github.com/skild-ai/newton/pull/25).
